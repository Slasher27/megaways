## How Big Bass Bonanza Megaways Works

Big Bass Bonanza Megaways is a six-reel slot on the licensed Megaways™ engine, where each reel shows a variable number of symbols and the ways to win are recalculated on every spin — up to a maximum of **46,656 ways**. Notably, that ceiling comes from six symbols per reel rather than the seven that gives many Megaways games their 117,649 figure, so the grid tops out slightly shallower than the format's headline number. Wins form when matching symbols land on adjacent reels from the leftmost reel; symbol height is irrelevant, and every payout is a multiple of your total stake rather than a line bet. You can read a full breakdown of the engine on our [Megaways mechanic hub](/megaways/) — what matters here is how Pragmatic Play feeds it.

The feeding mechanism is **Tumbling Reels**, Pragmatic's name for cascades: winning symbols are removed, the survivors fall into the gaps, and fresh symbols drop in from above, with the process repeating until a drop produces no new win. A single paid spin can therefore chain several payouts before it settles, and because the ways count reshapes with every tumble, a modest opening hit on a tall board can snowball. The mechanic matters most in the bonus, where it quietly does a second job — but that belongs to the features section below.

The betting range runs from **0.20 to 100.00** per spin, an unusually wide spread that lets the same maths model serve cautious bankrolls and high rollers alike. Given the high volatility, the low end is the sensible entry point: the game's designers built the big money into the free spins, and a bankroll sized for 100–200 spins gives you a realistic window to land the scatter trigger.

## What Are the Bonus Features in Big Bass Bonanza Megaways?

The feature set centres on one headline bonus round, a multiplier ladder inside it, and two random base-game modifiers — with no bonus buy anywhere in the game.

### Fisherman Money-Collect Free Spins

Landing **3, 4 or 5 scatters** anywhere on the reels awards **10, 15 or 20 free spins**, and the round transforms the game's maths entirely. Two new symbol types take over: money symbols — cash-value fish carrying a visible multiple of your bet — and the fisherman himself. The fish do nothing on their own; they land and wait on the grid. When a fisherman appears, he collects the combined value of **every money symbol currently in view** and pays the total at once.

The detail that makes the round work is persistence. During free spins, money symbols and fishermen **stick through the Tumbling Reels** — the regular paying symbols cascade away around them, but the cash fish stay parked, accumulating spin after spin and tumble after tumble. A long cascade sequence isn't just extra line wins; it's extra chances for money fish to pile up and for a fisherman to arrive while the grid is loaded. The gap between fisherman appearances is where the round's tension lives: the longer the wait, the fatter the eventual sweep.

There is deliberately **no bonus buy**. Every free-spins round is triggered organically through scatters, which preserves the anticipation of the natural trigger but also means dry stretches in the base game have no escape hatch.

### Multiplier Retriggers: the Climb to x10

The Megaways edition's biggest upgrade over the original game is its retrigger ladder, which counts every fisherman you collect during the bonus. Each time the tally reaches **four fishermen**, three things happen at once: **10 extra free spins** are added, the collect multiplier climbs a tier, and the counter resets. The tiers run **x2, then x3, then x10** — and after the third retrigger the multiplier locks at x10 with no further retriggers available.

That progression is where the 4,000x ceiling becomes reachable. The multiplier applies to fisherman collects, so it compounds with the persistence mechanic: a grid holding money symbols worth 40x your bet at the x10 tier pays 400x from a single sweep. Chaining collects like that across an extended round is the game's endgame — and the reason every angler that lands feels meaningful twice over, once as a payout and once as progress toward the next tier.

### Bazooka and Dynamite Random Modifiers

Two random modifiers keep the base game from feeling like a waiting room. The **Bazooka** fires after a tumble that fails to produce a win, rearranging the symbols on the grid in an attempt to manufacture a fresh combination — effectively a second chance bolted onto a dead cascade. The **Dynamite** modifier randomly adds money symbols to the reels; in the base game these fish mostly build atmosphere, since the fisherman's collecting power only ignites inside the free spins, which is the honest limit of both modifiers. Neither requires any input, and neither can be timed or influenced.

## Symbols & What They Pay

The paytable splits into two thematic tiers, with all wins paid as multiples of your total stake. The premiums are drawn from the angler's kit — the **fisherman** himself, the tackle box, the fishing reel and the rod-and-lure — and deliver the strongest base-game line wins when several connect across adjacent reels. Below them, colourful playing-card royals from **10 through A** appear most frequently and form the backbone of regular tumble chains.

Three symbols sit outside the line-pay structure entirely. The **scatter** (the bait box) exists only to trigger free spins. The **money symbols** — cash-laden bass — carry visible bet-multiple values but pay nothing as line symbols, activating only when collected. And the fisherman doubles as the collector during the bonus. That structure explains the game's payout profile in one sentence: because its most important symbols don't pay on lines, the bulk of Big Bass Bonanza Megaways' win potential is deliberately concentrated in the free spins rather than the base game.

## Final Verdict — Should You Play Big Bass Bonanza Megaways?

Big Bass Bonanza Megaways succeeds at exactly what it set out to do: transplant the franchise's money-collect ritual onto a variable-ways grid without losing the charm that made the original a phenomenon. The combination is genuinely stronger than the sum of its parts — tumbles extend spins, persistence turns those extended spins into stacked cash fish, and the retrigger ladder gives every fisherman a second purpose. Add the hand-drawn lakeside artwork, that inescapable soundtrack, and a stake range that starts at 0.20, and it's easy to see why this edition earns its **4.3 out of 5**.

The flaws are real, though, and they cluster around patience. High volatility plus no bonus buy means you will sit through barren base-game stretches with nothing to do but wait for scatters — the Bazooka and Dynamite modifiers soften this but can't fix it. The **4,000x** ceiling, while honestly earned through the x10 multiplier, will disappoint anyone shopping by maximum win, and the money symbols that litter the base game are little more than decoration until the fisherman is unlocked.
